Why Validation First

Coding is the easy part to teach. Judgement is not: knowing what can go wrong, what matters to a player, and what "done" really means. For 3 months Faral validates and documents real releases across our live games — Postknight 2, Abyssfall, Postknight, Tiny Guardians and King's League Odyssey — guided by Hian (QA lead). That means reading changelogs, breaking builds from the player's side, and writing up what she finds so a lead can act on it. She starts on a real release in Week 2: Postknight 2 v2.7.16. This is real work the team needs, not a warm-up. In Phase 2 she builds tools that fix the friction she wrote down in Phase 1.

    Working Standards

    Validation & documentation standards (Phase 1)

    Bug reports

    • Title: [Area] What goes wrong, when. Rejected if it's missing severity (1–3), reproducibility (always / sometimes / once), steps, expected vs actual, or evidence.
    • Severity 1 = blocks play or loses player data · 2 = wrong behaviour, workaround exists · 3 = cosmetic / polish.

    Test plans

    • Written from the player's view, starting from the changelog: what changed, what it touches, what broke last time.
    • Every plan covers happy path, edge cases, and failure cases. Hian approves before running (gate).

    Validation reports

    • Scope → results table → open issues ranked → go / no-go with reasoning. One page.
    • Every claim has evidence: a screenshot, a log, or a clip.

    Reviews & reflection

    • All review comments use Conventional Comments (praise / suggestion / issue / question / nitpick).
    • End of every validation: 3 things that worked, 2 friction points, 1 suggestion.
    • No game or UPM code changes in Phase 1. Docs PRs (onboarding doc) only.
    • Bug logs live in one Google Sheet per game release. Reports and checklists are Google Docs linked from the sheet.
